Anchorage Police Seek Nissan Rogue After Downtown Hit-And-Run Near 5th Avenue and C Street Leaves Pedestrian With Life-Threatening Injuries

ANCHORAGE, AK — Anchorage police are asking for help identifying a driver and vehicle involved in a hit-and-run that left a pedestrian with life-threatening injuries. Investigators say the crash happened late on Sept. 18 near 5th Avenue and C Street, and the vehicle kept going after striking the pedestrian.

The pedestrian was taken to a local hospital for treatment. Police have not said anything publicly about the pedestrian’s identity or current condition beyond the life-threatening nature of the injuries.

Police say the crash happened around 10 p.m. Sept. 18

According to Anchorage police, the collision happened at about 10 p.m. on Sept. 18 in the downtown area near 5th Avenue and C Street. The location is one of the city’s busier central intersections, where traffic can include drivers, pedestrians, and other nighttime activity.

Police say the pedestrian was hit by a vehicle that did not stop. After the impact, the vehicle and driver left the scene, turning the case into a hit-and-run investigation.

Investigators have not released details about the pedestrian’s movements, whether crosswalks were involved, or what led up to the collision. Those details have not been made public as the case remains under review.

Investigators are searching for a 2017 to 2020 Nissan Rogue

Police say they are looking for a 2017 to 2020 Nissan Rogue that may have been involved in the crash. So far, investigators have not been able to identify or locate the vehicle.

That makes the public’s help especially important, according to police. Even small details about the vehicle, its direction of travel, or damage from the collision could help detectives narrow down the suspect car.

Anchorage police have not said whether they have surveillance video, witness accounts, or vehicle debris that helped them identify the model range. What they have disclosed is that the vehicle remains unaccounted for.

Major Collision Investigation Unit is handling the case

The Anchorage Police Department’s Major Collision Investigation Unit is leading the investigation. That unit typically handles serious crashes and other major traffic cases, including collisions that cause severe injuries or death.

In this case, investigators are piecing together what happened before and after the impact. Police have not announced any arrests or identified a driver.

Because the pedestrian suffered life-threatening injuries, the case is being treated as a serious priority. Police are continuing to gather information that could help determine who was behind the wheel.

What police want from witnesses and anyone with video

Anchorage police are asking anyone who recognizes the vehicle or knows anything about the crash to contact Sgt. David Noll at david.noll@anchorageak.gov. They also say anonymous tips can be sent through Crime Stoppers.

Investigators are particularly interested in anyone who may have seen a Nissan Rogue in the area around the time of the crash or who has dashcam footage from nearby streets. That kind of information can help confirm a vehicle’s route and condition after a collision.

Police did not provide a phone number in the public notice, but they emphasized that any information could be useful, even if it seems minor.

Hit-and-run cases can turn on one early tip

In serious hit-and-run investigations, the first credible tip can be the one that helps police identify a vehicle before repairs, cleaning, or movement make it harder to track. Anchorage police are now relying on the public to help close that gap.

The department has said only that the collision happened in the downtown area, that the pedestrian was hospitalized with life-threatening injuries, and that the suspect vehicle has not been found. For now, those are the main facts available while investigators continue their work.

Anyone who saw the crash or knows where a matching Nissan Rogue may be should contact police or submit an anonymous tip through Crime Stoppers.
