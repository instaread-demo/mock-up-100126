Preston former child refugee amongst those paying tribute to late Dame Esther Rantzen

By Nicola Adam

Editor in chief (North West)

Published 30th Sep 2026, 23:20 BST

‘My train was the last train’ Esther Rantzen, who has died, reunited one of Preston’s most famous residents with the man who saved her from the gas chamber.

There’s one Preston resident who is more than aware of the ‘remarkable role of the late Dame Esther Rantzen in bringing people together.

Lady Milena Grenfell Baines MBE, who lives in Preston, was just one of the 669 child refugees saved by humanitarian Sir Nicholas Winton when he managed to evacuate them from Nazi-occupied Czechoslovakia in WW2 - she escaped from the clutches of the gas chamber aged just three.

"My train was the last train," Lady Milena told the Post during a previous interview. "But the real last train, as Sir Nicholas used to say, never left. There were 200 children waiting to leave when war broke out and the borders were closed. Those children and 16,000 other Czechoslovak children died in concentration camps”.

That’s Life! presenter Esther Rantzen, who has died aged 86, welcomed Sir Nicholas onto the show to reunite him with some of the 669, mostly Jewish, children whose lives he saved - including Milena.

In a statement, the Association of Jewish Refugees (AJR), which supports Holocaust survivors, said Dame Esther holds “a special place in the hearts” of many of its members. In bringing Sir Nicholas together with those he rescued, the campaigner and broadcaster helped bring their story “to the attention of the world”, the AJR added.

The charity said: “The AJR was honoured to bring together members who had journeyed to safety on Winton’s transports – Lord Alf Dubs, Hana Eardley, Alexandra Greensted, Lady Milena Grenfell-Baines, Vera Schaufeld, Renate Collins, Emanuela Norton and Lia Lesser, alongside Rev John Fieldsend – to thank Dame Esther for taking their story ‘to a global audience’.

“Their final words to her feel especially poignant today: ‘Having stood up to thank him, we are now standing up to honour you.’

“Today, the AJR stands with them in honouring Dame Esther and the remarkable role she played in ensuring their story would never be forgotten. May her memory be a blessing.”

Dubbed “the British Oskar Schindler”, Sir Nicholas, who died in 2015 aged 106, arrived in Prague to meet a friend, shortly after Christmas 1938.

The capital of the then Czechoslovakia was filled with refugees after Hitler’s unopposed invasion of its German-speaking Sudetenland region earlier that year.

Sir Nicholas organised trains from Prague to London – known as the Czech Kindertransport operation – to help children get to safety before stormtroopers annexed the whole country.

Once the children arrived in England, he worked around the clock to find families willing to put up the then huge sum of £50 and agree to look after the children until they were 17.

In 1988, Sir Nicholas was invited to the That’s Life! Studio for the programme’s broadcast of an episode about his work, under the pretext that he was there to check it for accuracy.

But it was revealed during the show that he was surrounded by audience members whose lives he had saved, and an emotional Sir Nicholas wiped tears from his eyes as they applauded him in thanks for his extraordinary efforts.

He was finally brought together with hundreds of the children – including Labour peer Lord “Alf” Dubbs and film director Karel Reisz – in an emotional gathering for 5,000 descendants of the “Winton children”.

Sir Nicholas’s story was dramatised in the 2023 film One Life, which was directed by Slow Horses’ James Hawes and starred Anthony Hopkins as the humanitarian hero.

On Holocaust Memorial Day in 2024, nine of the refugees wrote to Dame Esther, through the AJR, to thank her for the role she played in bringing them together.

Their letter said: “Featuring the story of the Kindertransport on That’s Life! significantly helped popularise the unique history of the Kindertransport. It is our fervent hope it will never again be needed.

“At the end of the second episode of That’s Life!, you famously asked whether anyone else in the audience owes their life to Nicky. Having stood up to thank him, we are now standing up to honour you.”

Tributes have poured in

Tributes have been paid to broadcaster Dame Esther Rantzen who is said to have left an “extraordinary legacy” through her campaigning work and been described as “our sunshine” by her family.

The 86-year-old died surrounded by loved ones at her home in the New Forest on Wednesday.

The former presenter, known for hosting shows such as That’s Life!, established charities to help the young and old, and in recent years became a prominent voice in the attempt to legalise assisted dying in England and Wales.

Dame Esther was “the most wonderful mother”, a “cherished grandmother”, and someone who will be remembered for “her endless capacity for joy”, her family said in a statement paying tribute.

Her children, Miriam, Rebecca and Joshua Wilcox, said their mother’s “legacy of compassion and understanding will live on in her charities”.

Prime Minister Andy Burnham said Dame Esther had “changed countless lives and gave a voice to people who often went unheard”.
