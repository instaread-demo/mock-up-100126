EXCLUSIVE: Rhiannon Lambert on what kids really need to eat: ‘We’ve never had a protein deficiency in this country’

Ade Ilori
PUBLISHED: 09:00, 25 Sep 2026
UPDATED: 09:00, 25 Sep 2026

In a candid discussion on The Netmums Podcast, renowned nutritionist Rhiannon Lambert shares her insights on children's nutrition, emphasising the importance of fibre over protein. Lambert advises parents to focus on adding nutritious elements to meals rather than imposing strict dietary restrictions.
© Netmums
In brief
  • 🎙️ Louise Burke hosts Rhiannon Lambert on The Netmums Podcast to discuss family nutrition and the importance of fibre.
  • 🥗 Rhiannon Lambert advises adding more nutritious foods rather than imposing strict bans to improve family diets.
  • 📚 Discover practical tips to enhance your family's nutrition without stress by reading the full article.

This week on The Netmums Podcast, Louise Burke is joined by leading nutritionist, Sunday Times bestselling author and Rhitrition founder Rhiannon Lambert for an honest and practical conversation about family nutrition, the importance of fibre and how to make healthier choices without taking the joy out of food.

In this special three part mini series, we explore how to make family food easier, with plenty of practical advice and a host of incredible guests along the way. Join Louise as she uncovers why fibre is so important for a healthy diet, and crucially how to get it into your family’s every day food. 

If you’ve ever found yourself staring at a box of Coco Pops at 7am, frantically reading the back and wondering if your kid is ever actually going to get enough fibre in their life, you are so not alone.

One minute we’re being told to load them up on protein, the next we’re supposed to be tracking their 5-a-day, cutting down on sugar, panicking about Ultra Processed Foods (UPFs), and somehow finding the hours to whip up organic, home-cooked organic meals from scratch.

Throw in the morning solo school run chaos, endless after-school clubs, work deadlines, and the dreaded daily question: “What’s for dinner, Mum?” and it’s enough to make anyone want to throw in the towel.

Speaking on The Netmums Podcast the nutritionist and best-selling author dropped a total truth bomb: 'we need to stop overcomplicating things'.

Her message for busy families? 'Forget strict food bans and total mealtime overhauls. Instead, the secret is wonderfully simple... just add more in.'

‘Think: what can I add?’

For Rhiannon, making healthy changes isn’t about stripping away the treats your kids genuinely love.

“Rather than thinking, 'what can I take out?'” she explains, “Think: 'how can I add in here?'”

It’s all about stealthy upgrades. Think throwing a handful of lentils into a midweek bolognese, switching out normal pasta for wholegrain, popping a few cherry tomatoes on the side of a plate, or blending butter beans straight into a creamy mac and cheese sauce.

And if you rely on fish fingers or ready-made sauces? That’s totally fine.

Rhiannon might be a top nutritionist, but she’s also a busy mum living in the real world, and she's the first to admit she doesn't cook every single meal from scratch.

“I’m a nutritionist and I don’t have it perfect all the time,” she confesses.

Because let’s be honest: a "perfect" family diet that leaves you completely exhausted and stressed out isn't doing anyone any favours.

Fibre is the bit we’re all forgetting

Protein seems to be having a massive moment right now—even our kids are coming home talking about protein shakes and bars. But Rhiannon worries all the hype is distracting us from the real dietary hero we're missing out on.

“Protein has absolutely cut through,” she says. “But it’s detracted from the real messaging.”

Her main worry? Fibre. Shocking figures show that the vast majority of UK adults and kids aren't getting anywhere near their recommended daily intake.

And no, fibre isn't just about keeping things moving in the toilet department (though that's definitely a bonus!). It’s also crucial for feeding the trillions of tiny microorganisms living inside our gut.

Or, as Rhiannon likes to call them when chatting to little ones: their ‘tummy bugs’.

‘What have you fed your tummy bugs today?’

Trying to explain grams, daily targets, and complex nutritional science to a six-year-old is a quick way to get ignored. But telling them they have a whole universe of friendly little bugs in their belly that need breakfast? Now you’ve got their attention!

So, what do these tummy bugs love to munch on? Fibre-packed plant foods.

“Have you eaten a rainbow today?” Rhiannon suggests asking your kids.

It could mean beans, whole grains, crunchy veg, fruit, or a handful of seeds alongside the stuff they’re already eating. The goal isn't to create food anxiety or make mealtimes a battleground, it’s to make healthy eating feel like a fun game.

© Rhiannon Lambert

Ditch the tracker apps (seriously)

If the thought of logging every single bean your child eats makes you want to curl up in a ball, don't worry. Rhiannon’s advice is to bin the numbers and focus purely on variety.

Look at the week as a whole. Can you throw some chickpeas into a side salad? Swap white bread for seeded? Pop a handful of berries or a chopped banana alongside their usual cereal?

Small tweaks add up fast. As Rhiannon says: “Let’s start one meal at a time.”

Breakfast is the easiest win

If you’re wondering where to begin, Rhiannon says breakfast is prime real estate for a quick fibre boost.

Swap out sugary cereals for porridge, toss some chopped fruit on top, or pick up a box of bran flakes or shredded wheat instead.

And please, don't worry about making it look like a picture-perfect Instagram bowl. No chia seed art required here.

The same "add-on" rule works all day long. If your child is obsessed with something a bit less nutritious, don't fight it - just add a sidekick. A fresh apple alongside their favourite snack, or a spoonful of sweetcorn next to their pizza. Add, don't restrict!

...But don't load their plates with beans overnight

Before you go dumping three cans of kidney beans into tonight's dinner, Rhiannon has one very important warning: go slow.

If your family isn't used to eating a high-fibre diet, jumping from zero to hero overnight can cause uncomfortable bloating and wind (and nobody wants that on a school night!).

Plus, there’s another key piece of the puzzle parents often forget: Water.

“You have to up your water when you up your fibre,” Rhiannon warns.

If you're serving up more wholegrains, veggies, and pulses, make sure the water bottles are topped up throughout the day too. Start small, give everyone’s tummy time to adjust, and build up gradually.

Repeat after us: Perfect eating doesn't exist

The best bit about Rhiannon’s approach? She genuinely wants parents to stop beating themselves up.

Helping our kids build a happy, positive relationship with food is just as vital as getting greens into them. That means there is always room for the treats they love.

Rhiannon even admits to serving up Cheese Strings alongside fruit and fibre-rich foods. Why? Because if a Cheese String is what gets your kid to sit happily at the table and eat an apple, that is still a massive win.

“Nothing’s perfect,” she reminds us.

5 cheat-sheet tips to remember for dinner tonight

Overwhelmed? Don't be. Here are Rhiannon's top 5 takeaways to keep on your fridge:

  1. Add, don’t restrict: Always ask “What can I add to this plate?” instead of worrying about what to ban.
  2. Feed the tummy bugs: Keep kids engaged by talking about feeding the friendly bugs in their belly.
  3. Focus on the rainbow: Aim for variety across the whole week rather than stressing over single meals.
  4. Go slow and hydrate: Increase fibre bit by bit, and make sure everyone is drinking plenty of water!
  5. Ditch the guilt: Perfect eating doesn't exist. Small, easy wins are more than enough.

Remember

You don't need to transform overnight or turn into a gourmet chef. Just take it one meal, one vegetable, and one handful of beans at a time.

How do you manage to sneak a bit of extra goodness into your little ones' meals, or are you right in the middle of a serious picky eater phase?
