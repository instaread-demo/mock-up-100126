I Finally Found The Best Way To Check Texas Lottery Tickets

WesPublished: September 29, 2026
  • Share on Facebook
  • Share on Twitter

Until recently, I was the world's worst lottery player.

Listen to audio version of this article

Instaread

So how does someone earn the title of "World's Worst Lottery Player?" It's simple: my method of checking results. My strategy was always this—wait until the lottery announced there was an unclaimed jackpot, then check my ticket. Turns out, that's a great way to miss out on all the little wins along the way.

So what changed?

The Lottery Kiosks Were Driving Me Crazy

Let's start with this: I love the Texas Lottery kiosks...when they're actually working.

I've tried at least a half-dozen of them, and only one has ever been able to read my driver's license. It's a perfectly fine license—no cracks, no peeling, nothing wrong with it. So naturally my brain goes, "If this thing can't read my driver's license, maybe I shouldn't trust it to read my lottery tickets either."

Maybe that's irrational. Maybe it's not. Either way, I wasn't a fan.

So the other day I caught myself thinking, "Someone really ought to make a lottery ticket reader app."

Turns Out They Already Did

Well, as usual, I was late to the party.

Not only does an app already exist, but the Texas Lottery has one of its own. I downloaded it, and honestly, it knocks it out of the park. It has no problem scanning tickets, even the ones that have been folded up and rattling around in your wallet for weeks.

The best part isn't even how easy it is—it's what happens when you win.

Sure, winning is fun. That's obvious. But the app gives you a little burst of celebration. It lets out a cheerful "Yoo-hoo!" while confetti rains across your screen. I was honestly expecting more of a "Yee-haw!" since we're talking about Texas, but maybe there's a marketing department somewhere that has strong opinions on celebratory catchphrases.

Either way, it works.

Now I'm checking my tickets regularly instead of waiting for news reports about missing millionaires. It's actually kind of fun.

Well...right up until the app cheerfully informs you that you didn't win a thing. That part still needs a software update.

Lubbock Restaurants That Closed in 2026 (So Far)

From fast food to mom-and-pops, and from upscale to a breakfast burrito favorite, here is a look at Lubbock restaurants that have closed so far this year.

Gallery Credit: Renee Raven

google maps

The Roof (2252 Marsha Sharp Fwy W)

Despite 13 years of success, particularly with students, parents, and alumni of Texas Tech, The Roof was closed in January of this year after the new property owner, Texas Tech, declined to allow this tenant to continue with their establishment. No word yet if Tech plans to open anything there. For more on this closure, read here.

google maps

El Mitote Mexican (4525 Milwaukee Ave #600)

El Mitote originally opened in 2021, but closed in February of this year with a promise to eventually come back after a "process of transformation and remodeling." This location is now Outlaws.

google maps

F & F Japanese Grill (6616 Milwaukee Ave #400)

Lubbock's location of F&F Japanese Grill closed in April, which was unfortunate because they actually knew how to cook great tofu (a weird passion of mine). This location is now Rieu Viet Fusion.

google maps

Big Chicken (7411 Milwaukee Ave)

Big Chicken called it quits after just two years in Lubbock. For more on the Lubbock Chicken Wars click here.

google maps

Nora's Mexican Restaurant (417 Frankford Ave)

Nora's called it quits in August after a very impressive 25 years in business. It appears this space is currently for lease as of the publication date (9/2026).

google maps

Lillie's Place (6313 19th St)

Lillie's Place closed in August after the owner decided it was best for her family. The spot was quickly taken over by The Matador Cafe, which is apparently the same folks who owned El Charro.

google maps

Velvet Taco (2401 Glenna Goodacre)

Velvet Taco closed in early September, along with another location in Waco.It is marked as "temporarily closed" on Google, so perhaps it has a chance of coming back.
