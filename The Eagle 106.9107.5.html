ROCK MOMENT – ZZ Top “La Grange”

They gotta lotta nice girls

ZZ Top’s “La Grange” sounds like it has been rolling down a Texas highway forever. Billy Gibbons’ guitar starts with a quiet boogie, Dusty Hill’s bass and Frank Beard’s drums lock into the groove, and then the trio turns up the heat. The song appeared on the band’s 1973 album Tres Hombres, but its story began with a real place: the Chicken Ranch, a brothel outside La Grange, Texas. Gibbons and the band treated it as a bit of Texas lore, telling the story with a wink and leaving listeners to catch the meaning for themselves.

The recording came together almost by accident. Gibbons recalled that the band was waiting in the studio for an engineer to finish some work, so they began jamming. When the engineer signaled for them to keep playing, the groove fell into place. According to Gibbons, they had never performed “La Grange” live before that session. What began as a loose warm-up became the song that would introduce ZZ Top to a much wider audience.

The track draws on the blues boogie tradition, but its sound is unmistakably ZZ Top: spare, gritty and driven by the chemistry of three musicians playing together. Gibbons later said he used an older Stratocaster whose sound suited the shuffle. Even the song’s closing suggestion that the narrator “might be mistaken” has a connection to Texas music history; Gibbons identified it as a nod to Buddy Holly.

There is one detail worth clearing up. The Chicken Ranch closed in August 1973, shortly after Tres Hombres was released, but Gibbons said the band did not cause its shutdown. The song was meant as a celebration of a place Texans already knew about. “La Grange” later reached No. 41 on the Billboard Hot 100—just shy of the Top 40—while helping Tres Hombres become ZZ Top’s first major national breakthrough. More than five decades later, that opening groove remains one of rock’s most recognizable sounds.
