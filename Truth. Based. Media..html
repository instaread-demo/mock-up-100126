Three men in ski masks picked the wrong house, and the wrong woman.

Late Saturday night, around 11:30, at least three suspects in black clothing and ski masks crept up on actress Monet Mazur’s hillside home in the Hollywood Hills. She was inside with her dog. She saw them on her security cameras. Then she confronted them. The crew bolted before they ever got through the door.

That is the New York Post account, and it is the kind of story Los Angeles keeps writing because City Hall will not stop the people writing it. Mazur, 50, best known as Laura Fine-Baker on The CW’s All American, did not wait for a press conference. She treated her house like it belonged to her.

LAPD Hollywood rolled up after the suspects were already gone. A helicopter swept the canyon. Nobody was found. No arrests by Sunday. Footage from the scene shows Mazur outside with her dog while officers work the property. Pepper spray sat on the doormat. The precise shape of the confrontation is still unclear. The result is not. Three masked men decided an occupied house with a woman willing to meet them was a bad night’s work.

The house is a two-bedroom place worth about $1.5 million, tucked into the hills with a view of the city. Pretty. Isolated. Exactly the kind of address that has become a hunting ground.

The Hills Are Not Safe Because They Are Pretty

This was not a one-off Hollywood scare. It is the same movie on a different block.

In April, masked men choked and beat 70-year-old Budget Rent a Car heiress Margaux Mirkin inside her Lookout Mountain home, broke her jaw, and left with jewelry. That same month, former LAPD investigator Moses Castillo told Fox News the crews targeting ritzy neighborhoods are doing homework.

We used to think high-end neighborhoods were exempt from crime. That’s no longer the case. You’re not safe anywhere anymore, especially in affluent areas. These criminals aren’t looking at zip codes. They’re looking at opportunity.

August brought a man and a dog prowling the Hollywood Hills property tied to Nia Long. Earlier in the year, Dylan Sprouse tackled a trespasser on his own lawn after his wife spotted the man and called 911, a confrontation The Gateway Pundit covered when an actor did what a lot of residents now assume they may have to do themselves.

Reality star Morgan Stewart’s Beverly Hills house has been hit more than once. A knife-wielding suspect drew police fire at another Hollywood Hills mansion this month. Masked crews smash glass, strip jewelry, and vanish into canyon roads before a unit can climb the hill.

Cameras help. Choppers help. Neither replaces a homeowner who refuses to be prey.

Scripture is not foggy about the spirit behind this kind of work. “The thief cometh not, but for to steal, and to kill, and to destroy: I am come that they might have life, and that they might have it more abundantly.”

That is John 10:10, and it still describes Saturday night better than any crime brief. The men at Mazur’s door came to take. She made the cost of entry too high.

Los Angeles will keep selling the line that crime is a feeling, not a pattern, while ski masks keep showing up on $1.5 million doorsteps. Mazur did not wait for that sermon. She looked at the cameras, stepped into the moment, and sent three cowards back down the hill empty-handed. More residents should take the hint. The people in black already have.
